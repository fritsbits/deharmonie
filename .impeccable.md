## Design Context

### Users
Seniors (primarily 60+) in Brussels' Noordwijk neighbourhood, Dutch and French speaking. They visit De Harmonie for activities, affordable meals, and neighbourhood services (transport, cleaning, meals at home). Digital confidence is **mixed** — some navigate the site independently on smartphones or tablets, others are guided by staff or family. The interface must be self-evident and legible enough that both groups succeed without help.

### Brand Personality
**Warm. Toegankelijk. Community.**
De Harmonie is a neighbourhood institution — not a startup, not a government agency, not a charity brochure. It's a living community hub where people genuinely like being. The tone is welcoming and unpretentious, like a trusted neighbour. Never cold, never formal, never distant.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Warm, earthy palette**: Off-white (`#fbfaf9`), near-black (`#2c2826`), sage green (`#81b59c`), orange accent (`#eb6643`), medium blue (`#4679bc`). These are the established brand colours — respect and reinforce them.
- **Typography**: Nunito Sans (headings, UI labels) + Source Sans 3 (body). Both warm and humanist. Headlines bold/extrabold. Body at 18px+.
- **Photo-forward**: Real photos of visitors and the space communicate community better than icons or abstract illustration.
- **Light mode only**: No dark mode needed for this audience.
- **Anti-reference — NOT**: cold/corporate government-site aesthetic, sterile institution feel, trendy minimal startup look, cluttered text walls.
- **Reference feel**: A well-run Belgian community centre — like a boulangerie that also does social work. Familiar, warm, purposeful.

### Design Principles

1. **Legibility is non-negotiable** — Body text at 18px minimum. All colour combinations must meet WCAG AA contrast ratios (4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for UI components). Never sacrifice readability for aesthetics. Clear focus states on all interactive elements. No colour-only communication.

2. **Warmth through colour and texture** — The orange accent, earthy off-white background, warm photo tones, and rounded forms signal "people live here." Lean into this palette; avoid cool grays, sterile whites, or corporate blues outside the established brand blue.

3. **Scan-first hierarchy** — Users are looking for the next activity or a phone number. Key info (date, time, location, price) must surface immediately without reading prose. Use size, weight, and spacing — not decoration — to establish hierarchy.

4. **Bilingual without friction** — NL/FR content is equally valid. Neither language should feel like a translation afterthought. Language switching must be obvious and always accessible.

5. **Trust through consistency** — Consistent spacing, colour application, and typographic rhythm signal a reliable, organised organisation. No visual surprises, gimmicks, or trendy UI patterns that date the interface.
