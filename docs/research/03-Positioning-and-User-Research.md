## 1. WHY LOKALE DIENSTENCENTRA EXIST — The Policy Framework

### The Legal Mandate (Woonzorgdecreet, 2019)

A lokaal dienstencentrum is legally defined as a facility that "develops connecting, preventive, and low-threshold operations in and with the immediate surroundings of users."

**Four legally mandated objectives:**
1. Support users (priority: elderly, informal caregivers, vulnerable persons) to strengthen their **self-care capacity and social networks**
2. Strengthen **social cohesion** in the neighborhood
3. Support users to **remain living at home** in good conditions for as long as possible
4. Contribute to developing **local social policy**

**Three mandatory tasks:**
1. Provide information about and facilitate access to care and support services
2. Identify and signal needs to relevant neighborhood actors
3. Offer recreation and meeting opportunities through accessible activities

### What This Means for Messaging

The law itself frames the center's role as **empowerment, not dependency.** The website shouldn't position De Harmonie as a place that "helps people who can't manage" — it should position it as a place that **strengthens people's ability to manage, connects them to their neighborhood, and keeps them home and independent.**

### The Bigger Picture: Vermaatschappelijking van de Zorg

De Harmonie exists within a fundamental Flemish policy shift called "vermaatschappelijking van de zorg" — moving care from institutions into communities. The philosophy: vulnerable people (elderly, disabled, chronically ill) belong **in** society, not separated from it. Quality of life — not medical outcomes — is the measure of success.

Lokale dienstencentra are the **key infrastructure** for making this work at the neighborhood level.

### Funding Context

- Brussels LDC annual subsidy (VGC): €45,917.33 per center
- Programming standard: 1 center per 15,000 inhabitants
- Minimum 32 hours/week service availability
- Free, low-threshold access — no user fees for basic services
- Quality evaluations every 3 years, mandatory neighborhood analysis

**Source:** Departement Zorg (departementzorg.be), Codex Vlaanderen (Woonzorgdecreet 15 feb 2019)

## 2. THE CORE PROBLEM: LONELINESS IS KILLING PEOPLE

### The Numbers in Belgium and Brussels

- **Nearly 1 in 8 Belgian elderly** experience loneliness consistently
- **1 in 5 Brussels elderly** have no one to fall back on in difficult times
- **35% of people aged 75+** feel lonely sometimes to often
- Among 75-79 year-olds, loneliness has **doubled since 2017** (daily loneliness: 3% → 6%; weekly: 8% → 17%)
- **Belgium ranks worst in Western Europe** for increase in elderly loneliness post-COVID — a 30% increase among older isolated adults
- **21% of Brussels residents 65+** cannot rely on others when facing serious problems

### The Health Impact — The Numbers That Matter

This is the evidence that makes the case for why De Harmonie literally saves lives:

- **Loneliness is equivalent to smoking 15 cigarettes a day** in mortality risk (U.S. Surgeon General)
- Loneliness increases **dementia risk by 31%**, Alzheimer's by 14%, vascular dementia by 17%
- Social isolation increases **stroke risk by 32%**, heart disease by 29%, premature death by 26%
- Estimated **€3.2 billion annually** in medical costs attributable to loneliness in Belgium

### The Multicultural Dimension (Relevant for Noordwijk)

Brussels's Noordwijk is one of the most diverse neighborhoods in Belgium. For elderly migrants specifically:
- **Language barriers** limit access to services
- **Cultural differences** create mismatches with how care is organized
- **Unfamiliarity with the system** — many don't know what services exist
- **Trust issues** from discrimination experiences
- **Stress from migration history** — aging in a "foreign" country

**Key insight for the website:** The site needs to be genuinely bilingual (NL/FR) and culturally welcoming. The diversity of the team (which we documented in our first report) is a strength that should be visible and celebrated.

**Sources:** Koning Boudewijnstichting (kbs-frb.be), Vlaamse Ouderenraad, BRUZZ (Nov 2024), Vlaams Parlement

## 3. WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS — Evidence-Based Interventions

### The Top 3 (All of Which De Harmonie Already Does)

**1. Group-based activities with a shared focus (strongest evidence)**
- Effect size: d = −0.47 on loneliness across 27 studies
- Key success factors: target specific groups, educational component, involve elderly in planning
- De Harmonie does this: conversation tables (5 languages), creative workshops, memory ateliers

**2. Shared meal programs (strong social + nutritional impact)**
- The benefit is NOT primarily the nutrition — it's the social connection
- Participants make new friendships, create community, disclose difficulties, receive support
- Meals provide structure, routine, and a reason to leave the house
- De Harmonie does this: sociaal restaurant, daily meals, delivery

**3. Digital/internet training (medium evidence)**
- Teaching tech access helps overcome mobility barriers to social connection
- De Harmonie does this: digitale workshop

### What Makes Interventions Succeed

Research consistently shows:
1. **Active participation** of elderly in planning (not top-down programming)
2. **Educational focus** (not just socializing — learning something together)
3. **Group-based** (more effective than one-on-one)
4. **Specific interests** (art, languages, cooking > generic "seniors gathering")
5. **Regular commitment** (weekly/daily > occasional)

**Implication for website:** Don't just list activities — explain what makes each one meaningful. A "conversation table Spanish" isn't just language practice; it's a weekly commitment, a group of familiar faces, a reason to show up.

## 4. WHAT PEOPLE VALUE — The Emotional Core

### The Three Things That Keep People Coming Back

Research consistently identifies three primary values:

**1. "I'm part of something that matters" — Social contribution**
- Elderly people don't want to be recipients. They want to be contributors.
- They want to feel they have something to give.
- The old French homepage got this right: "les utilisateurs sont au coeur du fonctionnement."

**2. "I belong here" — Social connectedness**
- Not just "being around people" but genuine belonging
- Seeing familiar faces, being known, being missed when absent
- The difference between "attending a center" and "being part of a community"

**3. "I'm still growing" — Staying active and learning**
- Maintaining purpose, engagement, identity as a capable person
- Learning new skills (languages, digital, creative)
- Physical activity tied to autonomy ("I can still do this")

### The Hierarchy of Needs for This Audience

Someone might first come for the **functional job** (affordable meal, transport to doctor). But they stay and return for the **emotional job** (feeling valued, not being alone) and the **social job** (belonging, friendship, routine). The website should speak to all three layers — but lead with the emotional and social.

## 5. THE SHAME BARRIER — Why People Don't Come

### The Pride Problem

Research identifies why elderly people refuse services they need:
- **Fear of losing independence and identity** — in Western culture, self-reliance = worth
- **Fear of being a burden** — many actively feel they will burden others
- **Loss of control** — they've spent a lifetime being the strong one; accepting help reverses the dynamic
- **"I don't feel old enough"** — 27% of non-attendees say they don't feel old enough for "senior programs"

### How Organizations Overcome This

**Language reframing:**
- Avoid: "We help elderly people" → Use: "A community where people meet, eat, and do things together"
- Avoid: "Services for seniors" → Use: "Activities in your neighborhood"
- Avoid: "You need this" → Use: "You're welcome here"

**Structural approaches:**
- **Social invitation** is the most powerful tool — being invited by a friend beats any marketing
- **Seeing yourself reflected** — if participants see people like them, the barrier drops
- **Choice and voluntariness** — emphasizing it's about what they WANT to do, not what they need

### The Dignity Reframe

Rather than "help" vs. "independence," the most effective frame is **interdependence** — the reality that all people rely on each other. Accepting support is not becoming less independent; it's participating in the reciprocal nature of human relationships.

The website should never feel like a medical brochure. It should feel like an invitation to a place where people belong.

## 6. THE CAREGIVER — The Hidden Primary Audience

### Who They Are

The adult child (typically 45-65, more often women) who is:
- Juggling their own job, possibly their own children, AND worrying about a parent
- Feeling guilty about not doing enough
- Afraid of making the wrong decisions
- Searching online during lunch breaks and late at night
- Looking for something — anything — that will help their parent and relieve their own anxiety

### What They Need from the Website

**Speed and clarity** — they don't have time to explore. Key info must be findable in seconds:
- What do you offer? (one clear overview)
- Is this right for my parent? (eligibility, location, language)
- How do I get my parent started? (intake process, first visit)
- What does it cost? (pricing transparency)
- Can I trust you? (staff info, recognition, testimonials)

**Emotional reassurance:**
- "Your parent will be welcomed, respected, and engaged"
- "You're not failing by seeking support"
- "We support families too"

### Belgium-Specific Caregiver Context

- **30% of Belgian adults** are informal caregivers (mantelzorgers)
- 150,000 FTE equivalent of informal care labor
- To be officially recognized: minimum 50 hours/month or 600 hours/year of unpaid care
- 83% of primary caregivers are satisfied caring for relatives and don't want institutional placement
- But 44% report substantial emotional difficulties and 72% cite emotional stress

**Key insight:** The website needs a dedicated "For family / Voor familie" section. Currently it has none.

### The Referrer (GP, Social Worker, OCMW)

Professionals who refer elderly people to De Harmonie need:
- Quick service summary (one-pager or dedicated page)
- Contact info for intake/referral questions
- Opening hours and location
- Eligibility information
- What the center does and doesn't do

A "For professionals / Voor doorverwijzers" section — even a single page — would serve this audience.

## 7. USER PERSONAS

### Persona 1: Maria (77) — "I just want a reason to go outside"

**Background:** Widowed 3 years ago. Lives alone in a small apartment near the Noordwijk. Originally from Portugal, has lived in Brussels for 40 years. Speaks French well, limited Dutch. Children live in Antwerp.

**Functional needs:** Affordable warm meal (pension is small), occasional transport to hospital
**Emotional needs:** Misses her husband. The apartment feels empty. Needs to feel she still matters.
**Social needs:** Wants conversation, faces she recognizes, a place to go on Tuesday and Thursday.

**Barriers:** Pride — "I'm not THAT old." Doesn't know the center exists. Language (services are mostly in Dutch?). Fears she won't fit in.

**What brings her in:** A neighbor mentions the restaurant. She comes for the meal. She stays for the conversation table (French). Within a month, she's a regular.

**Website moment:** Maria's daughter in Antwerp searches "restaurant social Bruxelles" on her phone. She needs to see immediately: where is it, what does it cost, what hours, does mom need to register, is there French. She needs to be able to send her mom a link.

---

### Persona 2: Karim (52) — "I'm worried about my father"

**Background:** Second-generation Moroccan-Belgian. Works full-time in logistics. Father (79) lives alone in the Noordwijk since mother passed. Father is increasingly isolated, barely eats, doesn't leave the apartment.

**Functional needs:** Needs his father to eat properly and get out of the house
**Emotional needs:** Guilt (he can't visit daily), anxiety (what if dad falls?), relief-seeking
**Social needs:** Needs to know someone is looking out for his father during the day

**Barriers:** Father's pride ("I don't need help"). Father's limited French/Dutch. Karim doesn't know what services exist. Doesn't trust "institutions."

**What would help:** A clear, warm website that explains what De Harmonie IS (not a care home, not an institution — a neighborhood place to eat and meet people). Photos of real diverse people. Staff who speak Arabic would be a trust signal. A clear "eerste bezoek" (first visit) process that feels low-pressure.

**Website moment:** Karim searches "hulp ouderen thuis Brussel" at 10pm. Currently, De Harmonie doesn't appear. With proper SEO and a dedicated home services page, it could.

---

### Persona 3: Sarah (38) — Social Worker at OCMW Brussels

**Background:** Handles 60+ cases of elderly residents. Knows the Noordwijk well. Refers clients to various services.

**Functional needs:** Quick overview of what De Harmonie offers, who qualifies, how to refer
**Emotional needs:** Wants to know her clients will be treated with respect
**Social needs:** Wants a reliable partner in her care network

**Barriers:** Already overwhelmed with information. Will only bookmark the site if it's useful quickly. Has referred before but finds it hard to explain what the center does.

**What would help:** A "For professionals" page with a one-pager she can print or share. Clear intake process. Direct phone number for referral questions.

**Website moment:** Sarah is writing a care plan. She needs to add De Harmonie as a resource. She needs the correct name, address, phone, opening hours, and a 2-sentence description of services — all on one page.

---

### Persona 4: Jan (71) — Active retiree in the neighborhood

**Background:** Retired teacher. Lives with his wife in a house they've owned for 30 years near Antwerpsesteenweg. Healthy, active, curious. Looking for things to do now that he's retired.

**Functional needs:** None yet — he's self-sufficient
**Emotional needs:** Misses the structure and social contact of work. Wants to feel useful.
**Social needs:** Looking for a community, new people, things to learn

**Barriers:** "I'm not old enough for a dienstencentrum." Doesn't identify as the target audience. Might see the center as "for people who need care."

**What brings him in:** The activity program — especially the conversation tables (he wants to practice his Italian) or the creative workshops. He doesn't come for "services." He comes for activities.

**Website moment:** Jan's wife sees a Facebook post about the Italian conversation table. She sends it to Jan. He clicks through and needs to see a vibrant activity program, not a service catalog.

## 8. MESSAGING FRAMEWORK

### Core Positioning Statement

**Current (implicit):** "A service center that helps seniors with meals and care"
**Proposed:** "The neighborhood heart of the Noordwijk — where people meet, eat, learn, and look out for each other"

### Tone of Voice Principles

| Principle | Instead of... | Write... |
|-----------|--------------|----------|
| **Warmth over formality** | "Het dienstencentrum biedt diensten aan" | "Bij ons ben je welkom voor een maaltijd, een activiteit, of gewoon een babbel" |
| **Active over passive** | "Activiteiten worden georganiseerd" | "Elke week koken, dansen, praten en leren we samen" |
| **Together over for-you** | "Wij helpen senioren" | "Samen maken we er iets moois van" |
| **Specific over generic** | "Recreatieve activiteiten" | "Spaanse conversatietafel, country line dance, naaiworkshop" |
| **Human over institutional** | "VZW Buurtwerk Noordwijk" | "De Harmonie — al 50 jaar het kloppend hart van de Noordwijk" |

### Language Guidelines (Based on Research)

**Use:** older adults, older people, buurtbewoners, bezoekers
**Avoid:** elderly, senioren (in headlines), hulpbehoevenden, zorgbehoevenden

**Use:** join, participate, contribute, together, welcome, community
**Avoid:** services, help, care, patients, clients, target group

**Use:** "A place where..." / "Een plek waar..."
**Avoid:** "We offer..." / "Wij bieden aan..."

**Lead with verbs:** Eet mee. Doe mee. Kom langs. Leer bij.
**Not with nouns:** Diensten. Aanbod. Doelgroep. Activiteitenprogramma.

### Headlines by Persona

| Persona | Homepage headline that speaks to them |
|---------|--------------------------------------|
| Maria (77, lonely) | "Elke dag een warme maaltijd en een warm onthaal" |
| Karim (52, worried son) | "Een vertrouwde plek voor uw ouders in de buurt" |
| Sarah (38, social worker) | [Quick nav to services overview + contact] |
| Jan (71, active retiree) | "Ontdek onze activiteiten — van Italiaans tot country dance" |

The homepage needs to serve all four simultaneously. The hero section should lead with the emotional/community message, with clear pathways to activities, services, and weekly menu within one scroll.

## 10. THE ROLE OF FOOD — The Social Restaurant as Heart of the Center

### Why the Menu Page Matters More Than You Think

Research shows the social function of shared meals goes far beyond nutrition:

- People eat **up to 60% more** in the presence of familiar others — the social setting directly improves dietary intake for people who otherwise eat poorly alone
- Shared meals provide **structure** (a reason to get dressed, leave the house, at a specific time)
- The meal is a **low-threshold entry point** — someone might not come for "activities" but will come for affordable food. Once there, social contact happens naturally
- Participants report: new friendships, community belonging, a safe space to share difficulties

### Implication for the Website

The weekmenu page should be treated as a **primary landing page**, not a utility page. It should:
- Always show the current week prominently
- Include pricing with solidarity tiers
- Show the social context (not just "soep + dagschotel" but "elke dag samen aan tafel")
- Be shareable (families send it to parents, neighbors share it)
- Be printable (many visitors will print it and stick it on their fridge)
