# UX Strategy — Over ons (page-level)
_Status: Draft_

## Why this page exists

Over ons is the trust page. It exists to answer one question that families, referrers, and curious newcomers are silently asking: *"Is this a place I can feel good about?"*

Unlike the activiteiten and weekmenu pages — which are task-oriented — Over ons is purely relational. Nobody visits it for a specific piece of information. They visit it because they're uncertain, and they need the uncertainty resolved before they take the next step.

## Primary audiences and their mental states

| User | Mental state on arrival | Fear | Desire |
|------|------------------------|------|--------|
| **Family doing due diligence** (45–65) | Cautiously optimistic — they've heard about De Harmonie from somewhere, or searched for senior activities in Noordwijk | "Is this depressing? Institutional? A charity setup?" | Confidence to recommend to a parent. "I'd feel good sending my mom here." |
| **Curious potential new visitor** (65–80) | Mildly resistant — doesn't identify as "needing help." Arrived via a neighbour's tip or Facebook post | "Is this for old sick people? Will I feel out of place?" | To see themselves reflected. Real people. Normal life, not a care context. |

These two audiences have nearly identical needs. Both want **realness over polish** and **community over service**. Both will be put off by institutional language, charity framing, or anything that looks like a brochure.

## The page's emotional job

Make people feel: **"This is real, warm, and alive — and I (or my parent) would belong here."**

Not: "Here is our mission statement and organizational structure."

## The shame barrier, again

The same shame barrier from activiteiten applies here, possibly more strongly. The people reading Over ons are often at an earlier stage — they haven't visited yet. If the page feels like a senior center or a social welfare organization, it repels the people who most need to come.

The page must never:
- Use "onze doelgroep" framing
- Lead with governance or organizational structure
- Feel like an NGO annual report

## The 50-year story as an asset, not a credential

"50 jaar" is in the hero. That's good. But the story must be told as a **neighborhood story**, not an institutional milestone. The angle: *De Harmonie was always just the place where people in the Noordwijk came together.* That's still true today.

## What trust looks like for this audience

For families and curious newcomers, trust is built by:
1. **Real photos of real people** — not stock images, not empty rooms
2. **Voices of actual visitors** — quotes from Google reviews carry more weight than any copy we write
3. **A sense of scale and variety** — not a small volunteer project, but a real, staffed, daily-running operation
4. **Warmth of the team** — De Harmonie has a genuinely diverse, dedicated team. That's visible in Wie is wie. Over ons should gesture toward this warmth.

## Two-stage strategy

**Stage 1 (now):** Build a finished, warm page using narrative prose + existing photos + Google review quotes. No placeholders. Feels complete.

**Stage 2 (after jaarverslag, ~April 2026):** Add impact numbers, possibly a formal mission statement, potentially an annual report link. The page's structure should accommodate these additions without redesign.
